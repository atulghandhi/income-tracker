How to budget without linking your bank account

By The Income Tracker · Updated 16 June 2026

The short answer

You can budget without open banking by downloading a CSV of your transactions from your bank and importing it into a local-first tool like The Income Tracker. Nothing connects to your bank, no credentials are shared, and your data stays in your browser.

Why avoid linking your bank?

Most budgeting apps ask you to connect your bank through open banking (Plaid, TrueLayer and similar). That gives a third party ongoing read access to your accounts. Plenty of people are reasonably uncomfortable with that — they'd rather not hand a continuous feed of their finances to another company.

The good news: you don't have to. Every UK bank lets you download your own transactions as a CSV file, and that's all a budgeting tool actually needs.

The CSV method, in three steps

It takes about two minutes:

  1. Download a CSV of your transactions from your bank's website or app.
  2. Import the CSV into The Income Tracker — it reads the dates, descriptions and amounts automatically.
  3. Categorise anything you like by dragging it, and watch your monthly surplus update live.

Is this less convenient than open banking?

Slightly — you download a file once a month instead of it syncing automatically. In return you give up nothing: no third party can see your accounts, there's no connection to break or re-authorise, and it works across every bank you use. For most people that's a trade worth making.

Frequently asked questions

Can you budget without open banking?
Yes. Download a CSV of your transactions from your bank and import it into a budgeting tool such as The Income Tracker. No open-banking connection or bank login is required.
Is it safe to budget with a CSV instead of connecting my bank?
It's safer in the sense that no third party gets ongoing access to your accounts. You export the file yourself, and with a local-first tool like The Income Tracker the data stays in your own browser.
Which UK banks let you export a CSV?
All the major ones — Barclays, HSBC, Monzo, Starling, Lloyds, NatWest, Santander and Revolut among them. The Income Tracker has a step-by-step CSV guide for each.
