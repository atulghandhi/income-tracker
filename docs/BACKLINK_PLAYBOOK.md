# Backlink playbook

Written 21 November 2026. At that date SE Ranking found **4 backlinks from 4 domains** (domain rank 8/100). That, not the pages, is what keeps head terms like "expense tracker" (position 19) and "budget apps uk" (61) off page one. See `SEO_PLAYBOOK.md`, 21 November section.

The goal for the next three months is **20+ referring domains, at least 5 of them editorial** (a person chose to link: a university page, a blog, a news article). Track progress in SE Ranking or Bing Webmaster Tools (Backlinks report, free).

## Ground rules

- **Never buy links, swap links in bulk, or use "submit to 500 directories" gigs.** Google's spam policies treat these as link schemes, and a penalty on a young domain is worse than no links.
- **Always say it is your own product.** Every list and community below expects that, and some (awesome-privacy, Hacker News) require it.
- **Lead with what helps them**, not what you want. A dead app in their list is their problem; you are offering the fix.
- **Do not drop links in Reddit or MoneySavingExpert threads.** Both ban self-promotion, the links are nofollow, and a ban costs you a channel you may want later.
- **About nofollow:** directory, GitHub, Hacker News and Product Hunt links mostly carry `nofollow`. They still count for something (Google treats it as a hint), and they bring real visitors, some of whom write about you later. The links that move rankings are editorial ones from steps 2, 3 and 6.

Keep a simple tracker (a spreadsheet is fine): date, site, page URL, contact, what you sent, reply, link live (Y/N), follow-up date.

## Step 1: profiles and listings (one afternoon, this week)

> **One-liner:** A free UK income and expense tracker that works from your bank's CSV or PDF statement. No bank login, no account, data stays in your browser. Open source (MIT).

2. **AlternativeTo** (alternativeto.net): add The Income Tracker as an alternative to **Emma, Snoop, YNAB, Money Dashboard and Moneyhub**. The last two are closed, so people still landing on those pages are actively looking for a replacement.
3. **OpenAlternative** (openalternative.co/submit): it lists open-source alternatives to proprietary software. It asks for the repo URL and the product you replace (put YNAB or Emma). Criteria reported: open source, maintained, in English. You meet all three.
4. **Product Hunt**: create the product page now; launch later (step 5) so the launch is not wasted on an empty profile.
5. **Bing Webmaster Tools → Backlinks**: note today's count so you can see changes.

## Step 5: Show HN and Product Hunt (week 3 or 4, after the above)

**Show HN** (news.ycombinator.com/submit). The rules fit: something people can try with no sign-up, built by you, and you are around to answer questions. Don't ask anyone to upvote or comment. That is against the rules and gets posts killed.

- Title: `Show HN: A local-first budget tracker that reads bank statements in the browser`
- Post the site URL, then add a first comment straight away covering why you built it, how the PDF parser rebuilds tables from text positions, what stays local, and what is not good yet. Write it plainly; HN punishes marketing tone.
- Post on a weekday morning UK time (US East Coast is waking up). Stay on the thread for the first three hours.
- If it gets no traction you may try once more a week or two later. If it does well, never repost it.

**Product Hunt:** launch a week after Show HN, so you have learned from HN's questions. Prepare the gallery images (dashboard, import review, a bank guide) beforehand.

## Step 6: journalists (now, then every season)

The Christmas calculator already ranks 6 and it is the third week of November. This is the window.

1. **#journorequest** on X and Bluesky (free): search it daily for budgeting, Christmas money, cost of living and student money requests. Reply only when you can answer what was asked, with a specific tip and one link.
2. **Qwoted** (free plan): create an expert profile as "founder of a UK budgeting tool", topics personal finance, budgeting, open banking, data privacy.
3. **ResponseSource** is free for journalists but paid for experts. Skip it until the free channels work.

What journalists can use from you:
- "How much to put aside each payday for Christmas" (the calculator does this)
- Which UK banks let you export your own data and how far back. You have checked 28 banks against their own help pages, which few sites have done.
- The privacy angle on budgeting apps that ask for your bank login.

**Next seasons:** January (new year budgets, Blue Monday), late January (Self Assessment deadline: your countdown checklist), April (new tax year), September (students starting uni: the template and step 2's pages).
